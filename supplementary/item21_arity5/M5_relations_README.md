# Item 21, M5 — completeness lemma (a fragment) + a ruled-out shortcut (the `n=4` control)

*Two findings (2026-06-23, `m5_relations.py`), one positive, one negative — the negative is the more
instructive, and it sharpens what item 21 actually claims.*

## Positive: the completeness lemma (a ray-level first fundamental theorem)

Generalizing M4's structural observation:

> **Lemma.** On the generic stratum `(2n,2n,0)` (rays span `F₂^{2n}`), two labeled ray-tuples with the
> same ray-Gram `G` (`G_{ij}=ω(r_i,r_j)`) and the same relation space `R={a:⊕_{i∈a}r_i=0}` are
> `Sp`-equivalent. *Proof:* same `R` ⟹ `r_i↦r_i'` is well-defined & linear; spanning ⟹ bijective;
> same `G` ⟹ it preserves `ω` on a spanning set ⟹ it lies in `Sp(2n,F₂)`. ∎

So `(G,R)` is a **complete `Sp`-invariant** of the rays: every `Sp`-invariant of the 15 rays is a
function of `(G,R)`. This is a genuine fragment of M5 — a **first fundamental theorem at the ray
level** (the ray-invariants are generated by the Gram together with the relation space). Validated:
`(G,R)` is `Sp`-invariant under random transvections (which generate `Sp` and preserve `ω`) —
**140/140** across `n=4,5,6`.

## Negative: "`R` is arity-≤4-generated" is FALSE — and the `n=4` control shows why it doesn't matter

`G` is arity-≤4 (each entry `ω(r_{ij},r_{kl})` touches ≤4 Lagrangians). The tempting next step:
if `R` were also arity-≤4-generated, *every* invariant would be reducible and item 21 would hold on
the generic stratum (a vast strengthening of M4 — the whole ring, not just Arf). **It is not.**

Define `R₄` = span over all 15 four-subsets `T` of the relations among the 6 rays `r_{ij}` (`i,j∈T`)
— each an arity-≤4 (4-Lagrangian-local) relation. Always `R₄⊆R`. The **global-relation defect**
`dim R − dim R₄`:

| `n` | stratum | defect `dim R − dim R₄` |
|----|----|----|
| 4 | `(8,0)` all | **7 always** (`dim R=7`, `dim R₄=0` — *no* 4-local relation exists) |
| 5 | mostly `(10,0)` | `{0:9, 1:24, 2:69, 3:88, 4:43, 5:16, 6:1}` |
| 6 | mostly `(12,0)` | `{0:7, 1:22, 2:72, 3:93, 4:6}` |

The defect is nonzero almost everywhere — `R` carries genuine ≥5-index ("global") relations not
generated by 4-local ones. At `n=4` it is *maximal* (all 7 relations are global; 6 rays in 8 dims are
generically independent so `R₄=0`).

**The `n=4` control is the whole point.** `n=4` is **settled** — the master theorem gives **no `H⁴`
escape** there. Yet its relation space is *entirely* global (defect 7/7). So:

> global relations (= genuine arity-5 invariants) **exist even where `H⁴` is provably 0**.

Therefore "`R` is arity-≤4-generated" is **not equivalent to item 21**, and the arity-reduction route
**does not decide it**. The literal "every invariant is a polynomial in arity-≤4 invariants" reading
of item 21 (ATTACK_PLAN §1) is **too strong** — it is false even at the settled `n=4`. The correct,
operative content of item 21 is **cohomological**:

> the 4-cochain assembled from the arity-5 data is **exact** (no `H⁴` class) —
> proven for the `ω`-built cochain (M1, Paper XXII `thm:trunc`) and for the intrinsic Arf (M4).

This is **another shortcut ruled out**, with `n=4` as the clean control — directly analogous to
`co_reduce.py` (the C-O mod-2 reduction shortcut). It does not weaken item 21; it re-grounds it.

## Net effect on the program

- **M5 stays cohomological and paper-scale.** There is no finite arity-reduction that closes it; the
  global relations are real arity-5 invariants, and the theorem is that they assemble to exact
  cochains, not that they are absent.
- **Salvaged positive:** the completeness lemma is a real partial-M5 result (ray-level FFT:
  invariants generated by `(G,R)`). The genuinely-open M5 is then (i) the *second* fundamental theorem
  (relations among `(G,R)`), (ii) the descent from ray-invariants to Lagrangian-tuple invariants, and
  (iii) the cohomological truncation — none shortcut by reduction.
- **Discipline:** the `n=4` control is the model for testing any future "item-21 reduces to X" claim —
  if `X` is also true at `n=4` (where item 21 holds), `X` cannot be the deciding content.

## Third ruled-out route: abstract-complex `H⁴` does not model item 21 (`h4_cohomology.py`)

The completeness lemma makes one more route *look* tractable: build the abstract `Sp`-invariant cochain
complex `C^k = {`functions of `(k+1)`-Lagrangian orbits`}` (basis = `(G,R)`-fingerprints, canonicalized
over vertex permutations; valid on all strata by Witt), with the simplicial coboundary `δ`, and compute
`H⁴ = |C⁴| − rank δ⁴ − rank δ³`. Claim "item 21 ⟺ `H⁴=0`". **It is the wrong model — the `n=4`
control rules it out.**

- Machinery is **sound**: `δ²=0` verified (`δ⁴·δ³=0`, all rows) at every `n`.
- **`n=4` control fails the model:** `|O4|,|O5|,|O6| = 1,2,3`, `rank δ⁴=0`, `rank δ³=1` ⟹
  abstract `H⁴ = 2−0−1 = 1 ≠ 0`, while the master theorem gives **no** `H⁴` obstruction. No arithmetic
  bug (hand-count agrees). The spurious class is the invariant *"is this `K₅` in orbit #1"*: it's a
  **cocycle** (pairs to 0 on every `K₆` nerve, `δ⁴=0`) so it furnishes **zero** per-config obstruction
  — no contextuality content — yet it's not a coboundary, so abstract `H⁴` counts it. **The abstract
  complex over-counts via orbit combinatorics.**
- The alternative reading `rank δ⁴` over-counts the *other* way: at `n=5` there are ~135 `K₅`-orbits, so
  a `K₆`'s 6 faces are generically all-distinct, and an orbit-*indicator* function pairs to 1 — a pure
  combinatorial "obstruction" that would absurdly "falsify" item 21 at every `n≥5`. (Also: at `n≥5` the
  `K₆` orbits are unsaturated — every sample a fresh fingerprint — so those numbers are doubly useless.)

**Conclusion.** Item 21 is *not* "`H⁴` of the invariant complex `=0`"; it is the **exactness of the
specific natural anticommutation-type arity-5 datum** (the generalization of `N_anti=Sq¹ω`). That is
exactly why the right tools are **M1** (`N_anti` exact, proven) and **M4** (Arf reducible) — named
natural candidates — not the abstract complex. This is the **third route the `n=4` control has ruled
out** (after C-O reduction `co_reduce.py` and arity-reduction above). The discipline holds again: run
`n=4` as a control; if a proposed model gives the wrong answer there, it is the wrong model.

## Descent: rays determine the Lagrangians **exactly at `n=4`** (`descent_gap.py`)

The completeness lemma is at the **ray** level; M5's actual target is **Lagrangian-tuple** invariants.
These coincide only if the rays determine the Lagrangians — and a verification (correcting an
over-optimistic "`n≤5` faithful" guess) shows the descent is faithful **exactly at the universal
dimension `n=4`**:

- Each ray `r_{ij}∈L_i∩L_j`, so `L_i` gets exactly `N−1=5` isotropic rays; `K_i=span{r_{ij}:j}` has
  `dim ≤ min(5,n)` and pins `L_i` only when `dim K_i=n`. The Lagrangians containing `K_i` number
  `∏_{t=1}^{n−dim K_i}(2^t+1)` (Lagrangians of `K_i^⊥/K_i`).

| `n` | `dim K_i` | rays determine `L_i`? |
|----|----|----|
| 4 | `4` always (1200/1200) | **yes, every config** |
| 5 | `{3,4,5}`, `=5` only 556/1200 | **mostly no** (~46%) — the 5 rays are usually *dependent* |
| 6 | `{3,4,5}`, never `6` (0/720) | **never** (multiplicity `{3,15,135}` confirmed) |

So the ray↔Lagrangian descent is faithful **exactly at `n=4`** (where `H³` is exact and everything is
"free"), partial at `n=5`, never at `n≥6` — resonating with the series' "`n=4` is special." 

**Consequence (scoping, not a threat):** the ray-level completeness lemma is *strictly weaker* than a
Lagrangian-tuple FFT for `n≥5`; M5's descent step is genuinely lossy except at `n=4`. **But** the
framework's contextuality obstruction (`ω`, anticommutation, `n_a=Sq¹ω`) is **ray-level data**, so
item 21 is properly a **ray-level** statement — Lagrangian-invariants beyond the rays are a different
object and do not enter the `H⁴` obstruction. This sharpens M5's descent step; it opens no escape.
